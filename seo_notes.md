## SEO Fundamentals

### How Search Engines Work

#### Crawling

Crawling is how Google discovers pages.

Googlebot finds pages by following links and reading sitemaps.

Why it matters:

- If Google cannot crawl a page, it cannot rank.
- Important pages should be easy to reach through normal links.
- Avoid blocking important pages, CSS, JavaScript, or images by mistake.

Best practices:

- Link to important pages internally.
- Keep site navigation simple.
- Submit an XML sitemap.
- Check crawl issues in Google Search Console.

#### Indexing

Indexing is when Google stores a page in its database.

A crawled page is not always indexed. Google must decide that it is useful enough.

What helps indexing:

- Original content.
- Clear page purpose.
- No major duplicate content.
- Correct canonical tags.
- Important content visible in the rendered page.

Use Google Search Console URL Inspection to check if a page is indexed.

#### Ranking

Ranking is how Google orders pages for a search query.

Google looks for pages that best satisfy the user's intent.

Strong pages usually have:

- Relevant content.
- Good user experience.
- Trust signals.
- Useful internal links.
- Fast loading.
- Mobile-friendly design.

The core SEO process is:

```txt
Crawl → Index → Rank
```

### SERP

#### What Is A SERP?

SERP means Search Engine Results Page.

Modern SERPs are not just blue links. They can include:

- Organic results.
- Ads.
- AI Overviews.
- Featured snippets.
- Rich snippets.
- Map packs.
- Images and videos.
- People Also Ask.
- Knowledge panels.

Before targeting a keyword, always check the SERP. It shows what Google thinks the user wants.

#### Organic Result Parts

An organic result usually has:

- **Title link:** The clickable headline.
- **URL:** The page address.
- **Snippet:** The short description below the title.

These affect click-through rate. A clear title and useful snippet can win clicks even if you are not ranking first.

#### Organic vs Paid Results

| Type | Meaning | Main Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Organic result | Earned through SEO | No ad label |
| Paid result | Bought through Google Ads | Marked as Sponsored or Ad |

#### Common SERP Features

| Feature | What It Means | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Rich snippet | Result with extra details like ratings, prices, FAQs, or reviews | Can improve CTR |
| Featured snippet | Direct answer shown above regular results | Gives high visibility |
| AI Overview | AI-generated answer using multiple sources | May reduce clicks to websites |
| Knowledge panel | Entity box for a person, brand, place, or organization | Shows strong entity trust |
| Map pack | Local map with top businesses | Very important for local SEO |
| People Also Ask | Related questions on the SERP | Great source for content ideas |

### Main Ranking Factors

#### Relevance

Relevance means the page matches the search query and intent.

Do this:

- Use the primary keyword naturally.
- Answer the main question clearly.
- Cover related subtopics.
- Match the content format Google is already showing.

#### Authority

Authority means Google sees the page, author, or website as trustworthy.

Improve authority with:

- High-quality backlinks.
- Expert authors.
- Trusted sources.
- Real experience.
- Strong brand signals.

Avoid spammy link building. It can hurt more than help.

#### User Experience

Good pages are easy to use.

Focus on:

- Fast loading.
- Mobile-friendly design.
- Clear layout.
- Easy navigation.
- No annoying layout shifts.

#### Search Context

Google may change results based on:

- Location.
- Device.
- Language.
- Search history.
- Freshness of the topic.

This is why local SEO, mobile SEO, and updated content matter.

### Search Intent

Search intent is the reason behind a search.

This is one of the most important SEO ideas:

```txt
Intent > Keyword
```

If the intent is wrong, the page will struggle to rank.

#### Search Intent Types

| Intent | User Wants | Keyword Clues | Best Page Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informational | To learn | how, what is, guide | Blog, guide, tutorial, video |
| Navigational | A specific site or brand | brand name, login, website | Homepage or brand page |
| Commercial | To compare before buying | best, review, vs, comparison | Review, comparison, best list |
| Transactional | To buy or act | buy, price, discount, book | Product, service, landing page |

### E-E-A-T

E-E-A-T stands for:

- Experience.
- Expertise.
- Authoritativeness.
- Trustworthiness.

It matters most for YMYL topics: health, finance, legal, safety, and major life decisions.

Ways to improve E-E-A-T:

- Show author details.
- Mention real experience.
- Cite trusted sources.
- Display reviews or testimonials.
- Use HTTPS.
- Add contact and business information.
- Keep content accurate and updated.

Trust should be visible on the page.

### Google Algorithm Updates

Google updates its algorithm to:

- Improve search quality.
- Better understand intent.
- Fight spam.
- Adapt to AI, mobile, and new user behavior.

The safest long-term strategy is simple:

- Help the user.
- Keep content accurate.
- Avoid shortcuts.
- Fix technical problems.

## Keyword Research

### Audience Research

Before choosing keywords, know who you are trying to reach.

Audience research shapes:

- Keywords.
- Topics.
- Tone.
- Examples.
- Content format.
- Conversion offers.

If you try to target everyone, your content becomes too vague.

#### Buyer Persona

A buyer persona is a simple profile of your ideal customer.

Include:

- Age.
- Location.
- Job or role.
- Goals.
- Pain points.
- Search behavior.
- Buying motivation.
- Common objections.

Use real sources where possible:

- Reviews.
- Forums.
- Reddit.
- Quora.
- Customer calls.
- Competitor pages.
- People Also Ask.

AI tools can help find patterns, but verify the ideas.

### Competitor Research

SEO competitors are the sites ranking for your target keywords.

They may not be your business competitors.

How to find them:

1. Search your target keywords.
2. Ignore ads.
3. Note the websites ranking on page one.
4. Focus on niche competitors, not huge sites like Amazon or Reddit.

Study competitors to find:

- Ranking keywords.
- Strong pages.
- Weak pages.
- Content gaps.
- Common formats.
- Backlink opportunities.

Do not copy competitors. Use them as a benchmark.

### Seed Keywords

Seed keywords are your main starting topics.

Ask:

- What do we sell?
- What problems do we solve?
- What would customers search?
- What services or products matter most?

Start with 5-10 seed keywords. Then expand them into related topics and questions.

### Keyword Types

#### Short-Tail vs Long-Tail Keywords

| Type | Meaning | Pros | Cons | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short-tail | 1-2 broad words | High volume | High competition and unclear intent | `aquarium` |
| Long-tail | 3+ specific words | Clear intent, lower competition, better conversions | Lower volume per keyword | `best beginner freshwater aquarium kit` |

New websites should usually start with long-tail keywords.

They are easier to rank for and often bring better leads.

#### Question Keywords

Question keywords show exactly what users want to know.

Good sources:

- People Also Ask.
- Google Autocomplete.
- Related Searches.
- AnswerThePublic.
- Ubersuggest Questions.
- Forums and reviews.

Question keywords are useful for blogs, FAQs, featured snippets, AEO, and GEO.

### Keyword Metrics

#### Search Volume

Search volume estimates monthly searches.

Use it to compare demand, but do not chase volume blindly.

A lower-volume keyword can be better if:

- It is highly relevant.
- Intent is clear.
- Competition is lower.
- Conversion potential is higher.

#### Keyword Difficulty

Keyword difficulty estimates how hard it is to rank.

Beginners should look for:

- High relevance.
- Decent search volume.
- Low or medium difficulty.

This is the keyword sweet spot.

#### Google Trends

Google Trends shows whether interest is rising, falling, seasonal, or location-based.

Use it to:

- Compare similar keywords.
- Spot seasonal demand.
- Avoid declining topics.
- Plan content timing.

### Keyword Clustering

Keyword clustering means grouping related keywords by topic and intent.

Example clusters:

- SEO basics.
- Keyword research.
- On-page SEO.
- Technical SEO.
- Link building.

One cluster usually becomes one main topic or a pillar-and-cluster group.

This helps prevent random content and builds topical authority.

### Keyword Mapping

Keyword mapping assigns one primary keyword to one page.

Your keyword map should include:

- Keyword.
- Search volume.
- Difficulty.
- Intent.
- Cluster.
- Target URL.
- Status.

Rule:

```txt
One primary keyword → One main page
```

If no page exists, create one.

If multiple pages target the same intent, merge or separate them clearly.

### Keyword Cannibalization

Keyword cannibalization happens when multiple pages target the same keyword or intent.

Why it hurts:

- Google may not know which page to rank.
- Ranking signals get split.
- Pages compete with each other.

Check with:

```txt
site:domain.com "keyword"
```

Fix it by:

- Merging similar pages.
- Redirecting weak duplicates.
- Changing the intent of one page.
- Updating your keyword map.

### Keyword Tools

Use a mix of tools:

- Google Keyword Planner.
- Google Autocomplete.
- People Also Ask.
- Related Searches.
- Google Trends.
- Ubersuggest.
- Ahrefs.
- Semrush.
- AnswerThePublic.
- ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity for idea expansion.

Always verify AI keyword ideas with real search data.

## On-Page SEO

### Title Tags

The title tag is the clickable headline in Google.

It is one of the strongest on-page elements.

Best practices:

- Keep it **< 60 characters**.
- Use the primary keyword naturally.
- Put the keyword near the beginning if it reads well.
- Make the benefit clear.
- Match search intent.
- Avoid clickbait.

### Meta Descriptions

The meta description is the short summary under the title.

It is not a direct ranking factor, but it can improve CTR.

Best practices:

- Keep it **< 160 characters**.
- Explain the page value.
- Match the intent.
- Add a CTA when useful.
- Make each page unique.

Google may rewrite it, so the page content must also be clear.

### SEO-Friendly URLs

A good URL is short, readable, and descriptive.

Use:

- Lowercase letters.
- Hyphens.
- Logical structure.
- Keywords only when natural.

Good:

```txt
/knee-pain-treatment
```

Bad:

```txt
/page?id=12345
```

If you change a URL, use a 301 redirect.

### Headers

Headers make content easier to scan.

They also help Google understand page structure.

Use this hierarchy:

```txt
H1
 ├── H2
 │    ├── H3
 │    └── H3
 └── H2
```

Rules:

- Use one H1.
- Use H2s for main sections.
- Use H3s for subpoints.
- Include keywords only where natural.
- Keep headings clear.

### Internal Linking

Internal links connect pages on your own website.

They help:

- Users find related pages.
- Google discover pages.
- Pass authority.
- Build topical relevance.

Best practices:

- Link related pages naturally.
- Use descriptive anchor text.
- Avoid vague text like "click here."
- Fix orphan pages.
- Fix broken links.
- Avoid redirect chains.

### Image SEO

#### Alt Text

Alt text describes an image for screen readers and search engines.

Good alt text is:

- Short.
- Accurate.
- Natural.
- Useful for accessibility.

Include a keyword only if it truly fits.

#### File Names

Rename images before uploading.

Good:

```txt
knee-pain-treatment.jpg
seo-checklist.webp
```

Bad:

```txt
IMG_1234.jpg
DSC0098.png
```

Use lowercase letters and hyphens.

#### Compression

Optimized images make pages faster.

Do this:

- Use WebP when possible.
- Resize images before upload.
- Compress files.
- Avoid uploading huge images that display small.

### Video SEO

Videos can improve engagement and help explain complex topics.

Best practices:

- Embed from YouTube or Vimeo.
- Avoid hosting large videos directly on your site.
- Use a keyword-rich video title.
- Write a detailed description.
- Add relevant tags.
- Use a strong thumbnail.

## Content SEO

### Helpful Content

Write for users first.

Good content:

- Solves a real problem.
- Matches search intent.
- Answers the main question quickly.
- Covers the topic fully.
- Uses simple language.
- Avoids keyword stuffing.

There is no perfect word count.

Write as much as needed to satisfy the query. No more.

### Readability

Most people scan before they read.

Make content easy to scan with:

- Short paragraphs.
- Clear headings.
- Bullet points.
- Tables.
- Bold key terms.
- Images.
- Videos.

Every section should teach something useful.

### Keyword Usage

Use keywords naturally.

Good places for the primary keyword:

- Title tag.
- H1.
- Introduction.
- Relevant H2 or H3.
- Body copy.
- URL if natural.
- Image alt text if relevant.

Do not repeat the keyword unnaturally.

Use related terms instead.

### Topical Authority

Topical authority means your site covers a subject deeply.

Build it with topic clusters.

#### Topic Clusters

A topic cluster has:

- **Pillar page:** A broad guide.
- **Cluster pages:** Detailed pages on subtopics.
- **Internal links:** Links between the pillar and clusters.

Example:

| Pillar Topic | Cluster Topics |
|---|---|
| SEO | Keyword research, on-page SEO, technical SEO, backlinks, search intent |

This helps Google understand that your site has depth on the topic.

### Content Freshness

Old content can lose rankings.

Update important pages regularly.

Check for:

- Old statistics.
- Broken links.
- Outdated screenshots.
- Missing examples.
- New SERP features.
- Changed search intent.
- Better competitor content.

Refreshing a strong page is often better than creating a new similar page.

### AEO And GEO

#### AEO

AEO means Answer Engine Optimization.

It helps content appear in featured snippets, People Also Ask, voice answers, and answer-style search.

How to optimize:

- Give the direct answer first.
- Use clear headings.
- Add FAQs.
- Use lists and tables.
- Keep definitions simple.

#### GEO

GEO means Generative Engine Optimization.

It helps AI search systems understand and trust your content.

How to optimize:

- Write clearly.
- Cover the topic fully.
- Show E-E-A-T.
- Use structured sections.
- Cite reliable sources when needed.
- Answer related questions.

#### Inverted Pyramid

Use this order:

```txt
Direct answer
Explanation
Details
Examples
FAQ
```

Do not start with a long introduction.

### Schema Markup

Schema is structured data that helps search engines understand a page.

Use JSON-LD when possible.

Schema can help with:

- Rich snippets.
- Better CTR.
- Product details.
- FAQ understanding.
- Local business information.
- AI-friendly content.

Common schema types:

| Schema Type | Use It For |
|---|---|
| FAQ | Pages with visible questions and answers |
| HowTo | Step-by-step guides |
| Product | Product pages |
| Review | Review content |
| LocalBusiness | Local business pages |
| Article | Blog posts and articles |
| Organization | Company or brand information |

Best practices:

- Mark up only visible content.
- Use the correct schema type.
- Validate with Google's Rich Results Test.
- Do not add fake reviews or hidden FAQs.

### Content Optimization Tools

Tools like Surfer SEO and NeuronWriter compare your content with top-ranking pages.

They can suggest:

- Missing topics.
- Related keywords.
- Useful headings.
- Content structure.
- Word count ranges.

Use them as guidance, not rules.

Do not force keywords just to improve a score.

## Technical SEO

### Technical SEO Basics

Technical SEO helps Google crawl, render, index, and understand your site.

It covers:

- Sitemaps.
- `robots.txt`.
- Canonicals.
- Internal links.
- Structured data.
- JavaScript rendering.
- Hreflang.
- Site speed.
- Mobile usability.

Audit technical SEO after redesigns, migrations, and major site changes.

### XML Sitemap

An XML sitemap lists important URLs.

It helps Google discover pages faster.

Best practices:

- Include only important indexable pages.
- Exclude duplicates.
- Exclude blocked pages.
- Generate it automatically if possible.
- Submit it in Google Search Console.

### Robots.txt

`robots.txt` controls crawling.

Example:

```txt
User-agent: *
Disallow: /admin/

Sitemap: https://domain.com/sitemap.xml
```

Important:

- `robots.txt` blocks crawling, not indexing.
- Use `noindex` if a page should not appear in Google.
- Do not block important CSS or JavaScript files.

### Canonical Tags

A canonical tag tells Google the preferred version of a page.

Use it when similar or duplicate URLs exist.

Example:

```html
<link rel="canonical" href="https://example.com/preferred-page/" />
```

Best practices:

- Use a self-canonical on important pages.
- Avoid canonical chains.
- Do not canonical to blocked pages.
- Never combine canonical and `noindex` without a clear reason.

### JavaScript SEO

JavaScript SEO makes sure Google can see content loaded by JavaScript.

Common problems:

- Important content loads too late.
- Links require user interaction.
- JavaScript files are blocked.
- Large bundles slow the page.
- Lazy-loaded content is missed.

Best practices:

- Prefer server-side rendering for important content.
- Reduce unused JavaScript.
- Defer non-essential scripts.
- Test rendered pages in Google Search Console.

### Hreflang

Hreflang is used for multilingual or multi-country websites.

It tells Google which version to show to which user.

Examples:

```txt
en-US
en-GB
hi-IN
ar-SA
```

Best practices:

- Every language version should reference every other version.
- Include a self-reference.
- Use valid language and region codes.
- Keep equivalent pages connected.

### Structured Data

Structured data is the technical implementation of schema.

Use it to clarify:

- Products.
- Reviews.
- Events.
- FAQs.
- Articles.
- Organizations.
- Local businesses.

Use plugins, schema generators, AI drafts, or custom JSON-LD.

Always validate before publishing.

## Core Web Vitals

### LCP, INP, And CLS

Core Web Vitals measure loading, responsiveness, and visual stability.

| Metric | Measures | Good Score | How To Improve |
|---|---|---|---|
| LCP | Main content loading speed | Good LCP ≤ 2.5s | WebP images, CDN, compression, better hosting, critical CSS |
| INP | Page responsiveness | Good INP < 200ms | Remove unused JS, reduce plugins, delay non-essential scripts |
| CLS | Layout stability | Good CLS < 0.1 | Add image sizes, reserve ad space, avoid layout shifts, use `font-display: swap` |

### Platform Tips

| Platform | Common Problem | What To Do |
|---|---|---|
| WordPress | Heavy themes and too many plugins | Use WP Rocket, Rank Math or Yoast, lightweight themes, fewer plugins |
| Shopify | App bloat | Remove unused apps, use a lightweight theme, optimize images |
| Wix / Squarespace | Limited performance control | Compress images, avoid excessive animations |
| Custom sites | Poor rendering or asset strategy | Use CDN, caching, SSR, code splitting, optimized images |

## SEO Workflow

### Practical Process

Use this workflow for new pages and major updates:

```txt
Keyword Research
        ↓
Keyword Clustering
        ↓
Keyword Mapping
        ↓
Content Outline
        ↓
Write Content
        ↓
Optimize On-Page SEO
        ↓
Add Internal Links
        ↓
Optimize Images
        ↓
Add Schema
        ↓
Check Core Web Vitals
        ↓
Publish
        ↓
Submit Sitemap
        ↓
Monitor in Search Console
```

After publishing, track:

- Impressions.
- Clicks.
- CTR.
- Rankings.
- Indexing status.
- Core Web Vitals.
- SERP changes.
